    How renting works

    1. Download and start the renter app

      Unzip the kit and double-click Start CMFD Renter (Linux: ./start-cmfd-renter.sh). It sets itself up on your PC, including its own Python, and connects to Common Foundry's node. No account, no sign-up form.

    2. Fund your renter wallet

      The app creates a separate spending wallet and shows its address. Send it a few CMFD from your own wallet. About 3 CMFD covers a first hour; the exact deposit is shown before you confirm. The passphrase file it names is yours to keep.

    3. Pick a GPU and a template

      Choose a machine, then a template: PyTorch with Jupyter, a CUDA terminal, ComfyUI, Ollama, Qwen inference or vLLM. Confirm the quote. The app pays the host as the rental runs and renews only while you keep it open.

    4. Connect over SSH

      The app gives you an SSH command pinned to your rental's host key. Paste it into PowerShell or a terminal. Stop the rental whenever you like; what you didn't use is refunded to your renter wallet, and you can withdraw it back to your own wallet from the app.

    Beta. Hosts are Common Foundry's own machines for now. Renters reach them through a relay in Singapore, so throughput to a container is limited by that path while the beta runs. The app tells you what each rental measured.

    What you get

    • The whole machineOne rental per host: all of its GPU, CPU threads, RAM and the disk quota shown, not a slice.
    • Verified hardwareListings carry a signed test record. If a host fails a test, renters see it before paying.
    • Pay by the secondDeposits fund a payment channel. The host is paid for time served; the rest returns to you.
    • Keys stay with youYour wallet and SSH key live on your PC. The host sees only your public key.
